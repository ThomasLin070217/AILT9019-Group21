A place for each memory.

A working city-map module for the AILT9019 travel-album project.

What works now

Explore 19 cities, search English/Chinese names, switch Map/Album views and record repeat visits as separate cards. My local album saves real photos, dates and notes to local files and SQLite. Edit text or replace photos without adding visits; delete with confirmation; export saved cards as PNG; filter cards by date and back up or restore the album as a ZIP.

Optional AI drafts

The OpenAI-compatible Responses integration sends only the current trip after you review its contents and choose Send & generate. Results are editable unsaved drafts requiring an explicit save. Unclear or conflicting inputs can request clarification. Missing keys or provider failures leave the local album usable. This workspace now uses the course Bailian qwen3.8-flash model. Real generation and all five fixed cases have been exercised; independent human factual reviews remain pending. Simulated interface tests are recorded separately.

Try it

Choose Osaka, open “Try visit counting”, and add a sample visit. Edit a sample card: the count stays the same. Reload: the sample changes remain. Use “Reset sample album” to start over. All existing stories are fictional.

Data and privacy

The coastline and approximate city centres come from Natural Earth public-domain data. Two locally bundled example photos are credited to their photographers under the Unsplash License; they do not depict the fictional trips. With Google Maps configured, Map view loads Google’s online map services and their supporting assets. Without a key, or with ?map=local, the map uses bundled geometry. Our code does not send album photos or notes to Google. An explicit AI send uploads current-trip copies, city, date and notes to the configured provider, which may charge and retain data under its own policy. Real albums use a separate local storage folder; browser storage holds only demo records. Photo copies are converted to JPEG and embedded metadata is removed. ZIP backups contain your private photos and notes; keep them safe. API keys are excluded.
